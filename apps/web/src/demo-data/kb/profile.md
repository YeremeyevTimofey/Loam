# Profile

## Working context

I look after six client accounts at Northlight, a 20-person digital marketing agency. Acme Outdoor, Brightside Dental and Fernwood Foods are the biggest. My week is steady: client emails first thing, stand-up with the team at 9:30, client calls on Tuesdays and Thursdays, and the weekly reports go out on Friday. I work most closely with Marco (creative lead), Dana (paid media) and Pieter (finance).

## Style and tone

Warm, direct, no jargon. Short sentences. I use first names and British spelling. I never use exclamation marks with clients, and I put the numbers before the commentary. I sign off with "Best, Sarah".

## What good work looks like

A client can read it in two minutes and knows where things stand, what we are doing and when they will hear from us next. Numbers are named by channel. Nothing is promised that the team has not confirmed. My manager Lena reviews anything that goes to a client for the first time.

## Northlight agency handbook (pasted from Notion, 2026)

### About Northlight

Northlight is an independent digital marketing agency founded in 2014. We are twenty people in four teams: Accounts, Creative, Paid Media and Operations. We work with mid-sized retail, health and food brands on retainers of twelve months or longer. About seventy percent of our revenue comes from clients who have been with us for more than two years, and we want to keep it that way by being the agency that is easiest to work with.

### Team structure

Accounts is led by Lena and holds six account managers. Each account manager owns between four and seven client relationships and is the single voice to the client. Creative is led by Marco and covers design, copy and video. Paid Media is led by Dana and covers search, social and display. Operations is led by Pieter and covers finance, contracts, tooling and office management. Nobody outside Accounts speaks to a client about scope, price or timing without the account manager present.

### Client tiers and service levels

Tier one clients have a dedicated account manager, a weekly report, a monthly call and a quarterly business review. Tier two clients have a shared account manager, a fortnightly report and a quarterly call. Tier three clients receive a monthly report only. Response times: tier one within four working hours, tier two within one working day, tier three within two working days. Urgent issues, such as a campaign spending money on a broken page, are escalated by phone, never by email.

### Communication rules

Write the way a good colleague talks. Put the point in the first sentence. Do not bury bad news. Confirm every verbal agreement in writing the same day. Copy the client's day-to-day contact, not their manager, unless we have agreed otherwise. Never forward internal threads to clients. Never discuss one client with another. Keep a decision log for each account in Notion and update it after every call that changes scope, budget or timing.

### Brand voice and tone

Northlight writes the way a good colleague talks: plainly, warmly and with the point first. We choose the short word over the long one, we explain jargon the first time it appears, and we never hide a problem in the middle of a paragraph.

We lead with the result and the numbers, then give the reason, then say what happens next. Every message to a client should leave them knowing three things: where things stand, what we are doing about it, and when they will hear from us again.

We do not use exclamation marks, filler praise or hype words such as game-changing, revolutionary or best-in-class. We use first names, British spelling and the active voice, and we sign off with the account manager's name rather than the agency.

### Reporting standards

Every client report has the same shape so clients learn where to look. It opens with the headline numbers, split by channel. It then says what changed and why, in plain words. It closes with what we recommend and the one decision or action we need from the client, with a date. Reports go out by noon on Friday. Reports are sent as a short email with the document attached, never as an attachment alone. Numbers come from Looker Studio and are cross-checked against HubSpot before they are sent.

### Tools

HubSpot is the source of truth for contacts, deals and email history. Notion holds account plans, decision logs and meeting notes. Looker Studio holds the dashboards clients see. Slack is for the team only; clients never join our Slack. Google Sheets is used for budgets and pacing. Every client has a folder in the shared drive named with the client name and the year. Do not store client files on personal devices.

### Billing and invoicing

Retainers are invoiced on the first working day of the month, in advance. Media spend is invoiced in arrears with a five percent management fee. Payment terms are thirty days. Pieter checks every invoice against the timesheets before it goes out, and the account manager confirms the hours for their clients by the third working day. Queries about an invoice go to Pieter, never to the client directly. Credit notes need Lena's approval.

### Escalation

If a client is unhappy, tell Lena the same day. If a campaign is overspending, tell Dana immediately and pause it. If a deadline will be missed, tell the client before they notice, with a new date and what we are doing to protect it. If a client threatens to leave, call them within an hour and bring Lena into the next conversation. Write down what was agreed.

### Holiday and cover

Every account manager names a cover for holidays at least two weeks ahead. The cover receives a handover note with the status of each account, open actions and anything sensitive. Out-of-office replies name the cover and give a return date. Nobody is expected to read email on holiday.

### Onboarding a new client

The first four weeks follow a fixed plan: kick-off call in week one, access and tracking set-up in weeks one and two, first strategy presentation in week three, first report in week four. The account manager writes a client brief after the kick-off call and shares it with the whole team within two working days.

### Client playbooks

Acme Outdoor: our oldest account, since 2016, tier one. Contact is Jonas Weber, head of e-commerce. The spring and autumn campaigns carry most of the year's revenue, so budget conversations are sensitive in January and in July. Jonas reads on his phone: short emails, numbers first, one question per message. Creative approval goes through him and the brand manager, Katrin, and usually takes two days. Do not copy Katrin on reports unless Jonas asks. Acme's customers are outdoor enthusiasts aged thirty to fifty-five, they buy before a trip and rarely before a season. The strongest performer is paid search for waterproof jackets; email works best in the two weeks before a sale.

Brightside Dental: a chain of dental clinics, tier one since last year. Contact is Priya Nair, marketing manager. Healthcare rules apply to everything: no before-and-after claims, no promises about results, no comparisons with other clinics. Every piece of client-facing text goes through the clinic group's compliance reviewer before it is published, which takes three working days, so plan for it. Booking requests, not sessions, are the number Priya cares about. New clinics open in October and November and need their location pages ready one week before opening.

Fernwood Foods: a founder-led organic snack brand, tier two moving to tier one. Contact is Tom Okafor, the founder. He prefers a call to an email for anything difficult and dislikes surprises with money: any change to media spend needs his written agreement first. Health claims on packaging and in ads are regulated, so never write that a product helps with or supports anything. Fernwood pays late, so remind Pieter before every invoice run. Their online revenue is about thirty-eight thousand euros a month and the goal is to double it within a year while depending less on one marketplace.

### Meeting rules

Every meeting has an owner, an agenda sent at least one working day ahead, and a follow-up within an hour. Calls are twenty-five or fifty minutes, never thirty or sixty, so people can get to the next one. We start on time. If the owner is not there after five minutes, the meeting moves. Client calls always have a second Northlight person for notes, except weekly check-ins of under twenty minutes. Internal stand-up is at 9:30, standing, fifteen minutes. Notes go into Notion under the account, with decisions at the top and actions with a name and a date underneath. If a decision changes scope, budget or timing, it also goes into the decision log and into the next report.

### Approvals

Copy and images going to a client for the first time are reviewed by Lena. Anything that makes a claim about results, prices or health is reviewed by Lena and by the client's own compliance contact where one exists. Budgets above five thousand euros a month need Lena's approval before they are quoted. Discounts of any size need Lena's approval. Changes to a live campaign above twenty percent of daily budget need the paid media lead, Dana, and the client's agreement in writing. Contracts and amendments go through Pieter and Lena before they go to the client. Nobody sends a quote without checking the capacity calendar with Operations first.

### Social media guidelines

We write for people, not algorithms. Posts are short, specific and useful: one idea each. We answer comments within one working day and never delete a critical comment unless it is abusive; we reply with facts and take longer conversations to private messages. We use hashtags sparingly, never more than three. We credit photographers and creators. We do not post about politics, religion or competitors. For clients in regulated sectors, every post is approved by the client before it goes live, with no exceptions for urgency. We keep a calendar for each client in Notion with the post, the date, the channel, the approver and the status.

### Quarterly business reviews

A QBR is a fifty-minute call with the client, held in the last month of each quarter. The account manager prepares a short presentation of no more than eight slides: the goals we agreed, the results against them, what worked, what did not and why, what we recommend for the next quarter, and what we need from the client. We send the slides one working day before the call. We start with the client's goals, not with our activity. We end with three decisions that the client can make on the call. If a result is bad, we say so on the second slide, not on the seventh. After the call, we send the agreed actions within an hour and update the account plan.

### Crisis communication

If something goes wrong in public, such as an ad with the wrong price, a post that causes offence or a page that goes down during a campaign, the first person who notices tells Lena and the account manager at once, by phone if it is after hours. The account manager calls the client within thirty minutes with three things: what happened, what we have done to stop it, and when they will hear from us next. We do not apologise in public until the client has agreed the wording. We keep a written log of times and decisions. Within two working days we send the client a short written review: cause, effect, fix, and what we will change so it does not happen again.

### Freelancers and partners

We use freelancers for overflow design, video and translation. Every freelancer signs the confidentiality agreement and the data processing agreement before they see any client material. Freelancers never talk to clients directly. They work in the shared drive, not on their own devices, and their access is removed on the day the job ends. Work from freelancers is reviewed by the creative lead before it reaches the account manager. We pay freelancers within thirty days of an approved invoice. We keep a list of approved partners in Notion with their rates and their specialities.

### Expenses and travel

Client-related travel is agreed with the account manager's line manager in advance. We travel economy and by train where the journey is under four hours. Meals with clients are reasonable and are reported with the receipt within five working days. Software subscriptions are bought through Operations, never on personal cards. Equipment is requested through Pieter. If a client pays for expenses, we invoice them at cost with the receipts attached and no mark-up.

### Data protection

We handle personal data for our clients under their instructions and under the agreements we sign with them. We never export customer lists to personal devices. We share files with clients through the shared drive links, with access limited to named people and set to expire. We delete client exports when the work they were made for is finished. We report any suspected data incident to Pieter within one hour, even if we are not sure. Passwords live in the password manager, never in documents, chats or emails. Access to client accounts is reviewed every quarter, and access for people who leave is removed on their last day.

### Working hours and availability

Core hours are ten to four, local time. Account managers are reachable by phone for tier one clients from nine to six on working days. Out of hours, urgent problems go to the on-call phone, which rotates weekly between the account managers. Nobody is expected to answer messages in the evening unless they are on call. Flexible working is welcome as long as client commitments and the stand-up are covered. Remote days are agreed with the line manager a week ahead.

### Reporting calendar

Weekly reports go out on Friday by noon. Fortnightly reports go out every second Friday. Monthly reports go out on the fifth working day. Quarterly reviews are booked six weeks ahead. Annual reviews are in November and are prepared in October. The decision log and account plan are updated after every call that changes scope, budget or timing. The capacity calendar is updated every Monday morning by Operations and checked by account managers before any quote.

### Proposals and pitches

A proposal starts with a conversation, not a document. The account manager or the head of sales meets the prospect, writes down their goals in their own words, and agrees what a good first year looks like before anything is priced. The proposal then has five parts: what we heard, what we recommend, how we would work, what it costs, and what happens next. It is never longer than twelve pages. Prices are given as a monthly retainer with a clear list of what is included and what is billed separately, such as media spend, production and third-party tools. We show two options at most; a menu of five options confuses people and delays the decision. Lena reviews every proposal and Pieter checks the numbers. We send proposals as a link, never as an attachment, so we can see when they are opened and update them if something changes. We follow up three working days after sending, by phone first and by email second. If a prospect goes quiet for three weeks, we send one last short note and close the opportunity in HubSpot with the reason.

### Pitch days

For a competitive pitch we form a small team of an account manager, a strategist and a creative, with a named lead. We agree the win themes on the first day and write them on one page, and everything in the pitch has to support one of them. Rehearsals happen twice, once to check the story and once to check the timing. We bring printed one-page summaries, not the full deck. We arrive early, we ask the prospect what has changed since the brief, and we leave time for questions. The day after, the lead sends a thank-you note with the three decisions we would help them make first. Whether we win or lose, we write a short review in Notion: what we learned, what we would change, and who we should talk to next.

### Hiring and onboarding new colleagues

New colleagues get a first-week plan from their line manager: a desk and a laptop on day one, introductions to every team on day two, and a buddy who answers questions for the first month. In week one they read the handbook, shadow two client calls and write a short note about what surprised them. In week two they take over a small piece of real work with a reviewer. By the end of the first month they have a goals conversation with their line manager and agree what they will own at the end of the first quarter. We are generous with feedback and specific about what good looks like, and we ask new people what we could explain better, because they see our habits with fresh eyes.

### Renewals and annual reviews

Renewal conversations start ninety days before a contract ends, not thirty. The account manager prepares a one-page summary of the year: the goals we agreed, the results, the three best things we did, the two things we would do differently, and what we propose for the next twelve months. Lena reads it before the client sees it. The conversation happens on a call or in person, never only by email, and the renewal document follows the same day. If a client wants to reduce scope or budget, we ask what they are trying to protect, offer a smaller version of the work rather than a discount on the same work, and write down what would change in the results. If a client signals they may leave, we escalate to Lena immediately and agree who calls them. We never learn about a lost account from an invoice that is not paid. After every renewal, won or lost, the account manager updates the account plan, the decision log and the forecast in HubSpot, and tells Operations so the capacity calendar is correct for the next quarter. Small gestures matter in this part of the relationship: a handwritten note after a good year, a short video from the team when a milestone is reached, and a quiet word of thanks to the client's day-to-day contact, who is usually the person who keeps the relationship going from their side.

### Office, equipment and security basics

The studio is open from eight to six, and the front desk is staffed from nine. Visitors sign in at reception and are always accompanied. Every colleague gets a laptop, a headset and a second screen on the first day; anything broken goes to Operations through the help desk board, never by direct message. Laptops are encrypted and locked after five minutes, and nobody leaves client documents on a shared printer. Guests use the visitor network only. The meeting rooms are booked in the shared calendar, named after rivers, and are released automatically if nobody arrives within ten minutes. Lost badges are reported to Pieter immediately so that the access card can be blocked the same day.
