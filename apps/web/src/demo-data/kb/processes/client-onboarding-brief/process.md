# Client onboarding brief

## What we do

After the kick-off call with a new client I write a client brief and share it with the whole team within two working days. It tells Creative, Paid Media and Operations who the client is, what we promised, and what to be careful about.

## Steps

1. Read the signed SOW and the kick-off call notes.
2. Fill in the client questionnaire answers (below) into the brief.
3. List what we promised, with dates, and what is explicitly out of scope.
4. Write a short "handle with care" section: sensitivities, compliance rules, who decides.
5. Share the brief in Notion and tell the team in Slack.

## What a good result looks like

One page the whole team can read in three minutes. Facts, dates and names only. A reader who missed the call knows what to do on Monday.

## Constraints

Only what the client actually said or signed. No guesses about budgets or preferences. Compliance rules for healthcare and finance clients must be listed first.

## What it takes

Access to the SOW and my call notes, which I have. A human check by Lena before the brief goes to the team. Low risk if facts are taken from the documents; higher if the call was vague, in which case the brief should say so.

## Client intake questionnaire (pasted from Notion)

### Company

Full legal name of the client, trading name, registered address, VAT number and the date the company was founded. Names and roles of the three people we will work with most, and who signs off on spend. The number of employees and the number of locations.

### Goals

What the client wants to achieve in the next twelve months, in their own words. Which single number would tell them the year went well. What they have tried before and what happened. What they think their competitors do better. What they would be embarrassed to see in a report.

### Audience

Who the customers are, by age, location and situation. Why they choose this client and not a competitor. What worries them before they buy. What questions they ask the support team most often. Which words customers use for the product, which can differ from the words the client uses.

### Channels and tools

Which channels they use now, which they have stopped using and why. Who owns the website, the domain, the analytics account and the advertising accounts. Whether tracking is installed and who can verify it. Which email platform and CRM they use, and who administers them.

### Brand and approvals

Where the brand guidelines are kept. Who approves copy, images and claims, and how long approval usually takes. Any legal or regulatory limits on what can be said. Any words or topics that must never be used. Whether the client wants to see work in progress or only the final version.

### Budget and reporting

The monthly media budget, how it is split by channel, and who can change it. How often the client wants reports, in what format and for whom. Which meetings they expect and who attends. The date of the first report.

### Brand voice and tone

Northlight writes the way a good colleague talks: plainly, warmly and with the point first. We choose the short word over the long one, we explain jargon the first time it appears, and we never hide a problem in the middle of a paragraph.

We lead with the result and the numbers, then give the reason, then say what happens next. Every message to a client should leave them knowing three things: where things stand, what we are doing about it, and when they will hear from us again.

We do not use exclamation marks, filler praise or hype words such as game-changing, revolutionary or best-in-class. We use first names, British spelling and the active voice, and we sign off with the account manager's name rather than the agency.
